    A balanced plate

    Four simple ideas behind every recipe on this site.

    Half the plate plants

    Aim for vegetables or fruit to fill around half of most meals.

    Choose whole grains

    Brown rice, wholewheat pasta, oats and quinoa add texture and keep you full.

    Include protein

    Beans, lentils, eggs, fish, chicken and yogurt make meals satisfying.

    Flavour without fuss

    Herbs, spices, citrus and good olive oil bring big flavour with less salt.

    06

    Everyday habits

    In the kitchen

    • Batch-cook grains and roast a tray of vegetables at the weekend.
    • Keep frozen vegetables and tinned beans for quick meals.
    • Taste before salting; lemon and herbs often do the job.

    At the table

    • Start meals with a simple salad or vegetable soup.
    • Serve water with meals and keep fruit within reach.
    • Eat slowly and enjoy your food.
    07

    Questions

    What makes a recipe “healthy” on this site?

    We focus on everyday home cooking with plenty of vegetables, fruit, whole grains, beans and lean proteins, cooked with methods like roasting, steaming and grilling. We don’t make medical claims or count calories; for personal dietary advice, please talk to a qualified professional.

    Can I make lighter versions of my favourite dishes?

    Usually, yes. Small swaps such as yogurt for some of the cream, wholegrain pasta, extra vegetables or roasting instead of deep-frying can make familiar meals lighter while keeping the flavour.

    How can I add more vegetables without changing the dish too much?

    Grate carrot or courgette into sauces, stir spinach into soups and stews, and serve a simple salad or steamed greens alongside. Our veg boost tool gives ideas for specific dishes.

    Do I need special ingredients?

    No. Every recipe uses ingredients from an ordinary supermarket. Frozen vegetables and tinned beans are great, convenient options.

    Are these recipes suitable for special diets?

    Many are vegetarian and some can be adapted. Always check labels for allergens and seek professional advice for medical or specific dietary needs.
